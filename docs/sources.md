# MamaCare information sources

MamaCare links out to sources instead of presenting its own medical or facility database. The external services maintain their own content and may change it.

- [Clinic Finder SA](https://clinicfinder.co.za/) describes itself as an independent directory, not a government site. It says listings use OpenStreetMap and HOTOSM data and that the directory was last updated in April 2026. Its own site warns that hours and services vary and should be confirmed with a facility before travel. The app links directly to its province index (`https://clinicfinder.co.za/clinics`), verified province routes such as Gauteng (`https://clinicfinder.co.za/clinics/gauteng`), and its verified maternity-service page (`https://clinicfinder.co.za/services/maternity`) rather than copying facility records.
- The directory's [Free Maternity Care guide](https://clinicfinder.co.za/guides/free-maternity-care) is linked as a third-party resource. Its detailed service and clinical statements are not independently endorsed by MamaCare; users should confirm care decisions with a healthcare professional and current official guidance.
- [South Africa Department of Health](https://www.health.gov.za/) is provided as an official national public-health resource link. The home page surfaced general hotline numbers; MamaCare does not characterize those as maternity helplines.
- [World Health Organization, African Region — Maternal Health](https://www.afro.who.int/health-topics/maternal-health) provides general maternal-health context and is linked as an educational source, not individualized medical advice.
- [Western Cape Government — Know who you can call in an emergency](https://www.westerncape.gov.za/know-who-you-can-call-emergency) lists 10177 for ambulance and 112 for cellphone emergency. MamaCare shows those numbers with an explicit instruction to check local arrangements; it does not claim to provide emergency services.
- [UNICEF South Africa — Parenting](https://www.unicef.org/southafrica/unicef-parenting) describes its parenting hub as supporting parents in South Africa with child-development and child-care topics. The hub links to a [Child development guide](https://www.unicef.org/southafrica/unicef-parenting/child-development) that includes parent-facing articles about age-specific developmental milestones, including baby and toddler stages. The app links to the guide as an external source; it does not reproduce milestone claims or present them as an assessment.
- [Global Initiative to Support Parents — South Africa snapshot](https://support-parents.org/countries/south-africa/) states it was developed in November 2024 with in-country partners and is a snapshot of policies, stakeholders, and parenting-support resources. It was reviewed for resource discovery and is not presented in MamaCare as an official government service.
